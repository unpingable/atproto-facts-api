ATProto Observability Facts API

Public, anonymous, read-only aggregate facts about the ATProto network, published with the coverage, freshness and observation adequacy that qualify them.

publicanonymous read-onlyno API key CORS enabled

What this is

Several independent instruments watch the ATProto network and reduce what they see into aggregate facts. This API publishes those facts. It is a thin publication layer: it reads artifacts the instruments have already generated and serves them under a versioned contract. It does not run queries against any instrument's operational database.

The distinguishing property is that every fact carries the limits of the observation that produced it. How much of the estate was actually watched, how fresh the reading is, and whether the observation had enough standing to support a negative claim all travel with the data rather than being lost at the boundary. If an instrument could not see something, you are told that, instead of receiving a confident zero.

Quick start

curl -s https://api.neutral.zone/atproto/v1/instruments
curl -s https://api.neutral.zone/atproto/v1/labelwatch/overview
curl -s https://api.neutral.zone/atproto/v1/weatherwatch/conditions
curl -s https://api.neutral.zone/atproto/v1/weatherwatch/metrics

Conditional requests work; responses carry ETag and Last-Modified.

curl -s -H 'If-None-Match: "<etag>"' -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' \
  https://api.neutral.zone/atproto/v1/labelwatch/overview

Endpoints

PathReturns
GET /atproto/v1/instruments Which instruments contribute and whether each is readable right now.
GET /atproto/v1/labelwatch/overview Labeler counts, reachability census, alert counts by rule, and the estate-level weather verdict with its observation adequacy.
GET /atproto/v1/weatherwatch/conditions Plain-language reading of current aggregate activity, with freshness and interval coverage.
GET /atproto/v1/weatherwatch/metrics Per-collection event totals and mean rates, with observed and unobserved window counts.
GET /atproto/openapi.json Machine-readable contract (OpenAPI 3.1).
GET /atproto/health Liveness of this publication service. Says nothing about the instruments.

GET and HEAD only. Anything else returns 405. There are no write, admin, or per-subject routes, and there is nothing to authenticate as.

The response envelope

Every fact response has the same outer shape.

FieldMeaning
contract Stable identifier for the fact contract and its major version, e.g. atproto.facts.labelwatch.overview/v1. Pin against this.
instrumentWhich instrument produced the facts.
generated_at When the instrument produced the underlying artifact. This is the timestamp that matters for how current the facts are.
retrieved_at When this service read that artifact to answer you. Always recent; not a measure of data freshness.
age_seconds retrieved_at minus generated_at. How stale the facts are, in one number.
factsThe observations themselves.
caveats Limits that are part of the fact, not legal boilerplate. They state what the instrument structurally cannot see.
provenance How the facts were obtained, and an explicit does_not_measure list where the instrument publishes one.
freshness Present on Weatherwatch responses: the instrument's own freshness state and the threshold it was judged against.

Time and window semantics

All timestamps are UTC, ISO 8601. Three different times appear and they are not interchangeable:

  • Window — the interval the facts describe (facts.window.start / .end). Aggregate counts and rates belong to this interval and to no other.
  • generated_at — when the instrument computed the reading.
  • retrieved_at — when this API served it to you.

Windows are preserved exactly as the instrument produced them and are never re-cut at serialization. Weatherwatch rates divide by observed duration, not nominal window width; unobserved windows are omitted from rates rather than interpolated across.

Coverage, adequacy and caveats

This is the part worth reading before you use the numbers.

Coverage

facts.window.coverage_ratio is observed seconds divided by the span of the window. A ratio below 1 means the instrument was not watching for part of the interval. Counts are of what was observed, never an estimate of what occurred.

Observation adequacy (Labelwatch)

facts.observation.adequacy is one of adequate, partial, or unobserved, and it qualifies the entire reading. It exists because a window that saw nothing cannot report that nothing happened.

Negative claims are gated. When adequacy is partial or unobserved, network_weather.signals will not contain calm. It reports under-observed or unobserved instead, and network_weather.supports_negative_claim is false. Positive findings still stand — an observed spike was observed — but the absence of a finding is not published as evidence of absence.

Practically: if supports_negative_claim is false, do not read a quiet result as "nothing is happening". Read it as "not enough was seen to say".

Caveats

caveats and provenance.does_not_measure describe structural blind spots. Weatherwatch, for instance, counts events and not accounts, so participant turnover is unavailable by construction rather than merely unimplemented.

What is not published

  • No subject identities. No DIDs, handles or at:// URIs of the people or records an instrument observed. No stable identity hashes, and no groupings small enough to resolve to individuals.
  • No Driftwatch per-subject state. Driftwatch observes claim propagation per subject. That is private, is not reachable from the internet, and is not published here in any form.
  • No raw collector payloads, no database access, no query language, no filtering DSL.
  • No internal storage, build or deployment state.
  • No write or control surface.

Labeler DIDs are published, deliberately. A labeler is a public moderation service with a discoverable endpoint, not a person, and the instruments already publish these identifiers. That is the one identity-shaped thing here, and it is a service identity.

Publication is an allow-list: each field is selected by name. A new field appearing inside an instrument cannot reach this API without someone adding it on purpose.

Versioning and compatibility

Deliberately modest.

  • Within /atproto/v1, the meaning of an existing field does not silently change.
  • New fields may be added. Do not assume the set of keys is closed; ignore what you do not recognise.
  • Removing a field, or reinterpreting what one means, requires a new major version at a new path, e.g. /atproto/v2.
  • The contract string in each response carries the same major version as the path. Pin to it.
  • Any endpoint-specific exception to the above will be documented on this page. There are currently none.

/atproto/ is one namespace on a host that may carry unrelated APIs. Nothing here claims the root, and paths outside /atproto/ are not part of this contract.

Corrections and supersession

Instruments restate facts. A reducer gets fixed, or a window is recomputed as late data arrives. There is no separate correction feed: a re-read of the same endpoint returns the current best reading, and generated_at tells you which computation you have.

For two responses describing the same window, the one with the later generated_at supersedes the earlier. ETag changes whenever the underlying artifact changes, so a 304 means nothing has been restated since your last fetch. Facts are not immutable and should not be cached indefinitely as though they were.

Errors

StatusMeaning
304Your If-None-Match matched; nothing has changed.
404No such path.
405You used a method other than GET or HEAD.
503 The instrument's artifact could not be read. This is absence of evidence, not evidence that the instrument observed nothing.
